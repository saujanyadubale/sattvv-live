# v0.3 — Dynamic verification experiment protocol

## Research hypothesis

An online feedback-aware policy can select next test procedures more efficiently than a fixed catalog order or a strong coverage-aware predetermined plan, when faults are present and observable during the test campaign. The competing hypothesis is that adaptive scheduling loses under **delayed faults** when the policy uses each check once and cannot retest.

## Changes relative to previous stages

- v0.1 used isolated deterministic-ish sensor snapshots and **synthetic cost units** with 2.60 A EPS current rule.
- v0.2 used persistent spacecraft dynamics, a 1.30 A current limit, and a scripted command schedule; it **did not benchmark test-selection policies**.
- v0.3 uses persistent v0.2 dynamics with four v0.1-inspired policies, **test durations measured in simulation minutes**, mode-dependent procedure execution, a 10-second sampler, test evidence and health controls. v0.1 metrics and v0.3 metrics are **not directly comparable**.

## Hypothesis test configuration

- **Faults:** 10 distinct synthetic fault families from the v0.2 model, plus `none` healthy control. One fault per campaign; no mixtures.
- **Severity:** fixed at 1.0 for the pilot. Browser allows 0.0–3.0; future benchmarks must vary it independently.
- **Start:** primary onset at `t=0` and secondary onset at `t=600` simulated seconds.
- **Seeds:** 0 through 9 inclusive; paired by seed across policies for each fault/budget. Because different strategies take different trajectories, the emitted sensor-noise sequences need not match despite equal RNG seeds.
- **Initial state:** BOOT, battery SOC=0.80, temperature 23°C, solar illuminated, duration limited by the chosen testing budget.
- **Test catalog:** 12 named procedures; mapped cost `1 catalog unit = 60 simulated seconds`. Commands/mode changes are instantaneous. All physical state, including SOC, temperature and possible safety intervention, persists across tests within a campaign.
- **Time integration:** physical model stepped every 10 simulated seconds and sampled after each step. Fault onset is aligned to this interval. The evaluator records a failing requirement at the **test completion time**.
- **Early stop:** all policies stop the campaign after the first failed requirement. Rejected commands are expected for an illegal-transition guard test and do not count as failure unless the specified guard check fails.
- **Preconditions:** BFS finds paths using public legal transition graph. A blocked prerequisite yields `null`/INCONCLUSIVE unless reaching that mode is itself a test requirement (e.g., REQ-MODE or REQ-PAY).
- **Policies:** `fixed` executes catalog order; `random` executes a seed-dependent permutation; `model_fixed` computes a greedy coverage/priority plan ONCE, independent of telemetry; `adaptive` recomputes after each completed test, using achieved requirement/transition coverage and observed telemetry warnings.
- **Policy visibility:** allowed inputs are public test catalog, checks/actions obtained from selected tests, and telemetry. Prohibited input is the synthetic fault label/activation time/severity. These are in evaluator metadata only.
- **Budget control:** total completed-test cost never exceeds allotted simulated minutes. No inter-test restarts. Fixed/random stop when the next predefined test doesn't fit; adaptive selects an eligible test. That difference is a policy design choice and an important fairness limitation.

## Key metrics

- **Fault-case detection rate:** fraction of single-fault campaigns with at least one failing requirement check.
- **Healthy false-failure rate:** fraction of healthy campaigns with a failing check. A false failure on a healthy spacecraft is not directly an anomaly-detection false alarm: low SOC and environment can create genuine requirement breaches even when no fault injection was made.
- **Detection time:** simulated elapsed minutes until the **end of the first test with an observed requirement failure**. Not exact physical detection latency.
- **Campaign cost:** simulated minutes charged for completed tests before failure or budget end.
- **Requirement coverage:** number of distinct requirements actually evaluated (not automatically credited because a procedure was scheduled).

## Primary benchmark: fault active from t = 0

Seed count: 10 per fault family and budget, 100 injected-fault cases + 10 healthy controls *per policy and budget*.

| Budget (min) | Fixed | Random | Model-fixed | Adaptive |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 10% | 30% | 50% | 50% |
| 12 | 40% | 40% | 60% | 90% |
| 20 | 67% | 61% | 70% | **100%** |
| 26 | 67% | 79% | 100% | 100% |
| 33 | 97% | 96% | 100% | 100% |

All four policies produced a 0% healthy false-failure rate under these *specific* default healthy-control conditions, over 10 healthy seeds each. Sample sizes, synthetic fault design, and policy tuning mean the quoted 100% must **not** be generalized to real spacecraft. Do not treat seeded replicas as independent fault physics: much variation is just 0.1°C measurement noise, while fault effects often dominate.

## Sensitivity check: onset delayed to t = 600 seconds

Exactly the same evaluation grid, but faults are activated 10 simulated minutes after verification starts.

| Budget (min) | Fixed | Random | Model-fixed | Adaptive |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% |
| 12 | **30%** | 10% | 10% | 10% |
| 20 | **50%** | 34% | 10% | 20% |
| 26 | **60%** | 55% | 30% | 30% |
| 33 | **90%** | 74% | 70% | 70% |

**Finding:** relative performance is *not stable across fault onset*. The seemingly dominant initial adaptive policy can finish key checks before the fault appears and never retest those checks. A less efficient initial order may actually detect delayed faults more often. The immediate next research question is **when to repeat checks and how to allocate verification effort under changing spacecraft state**.

## Threats to validity / limitations

1. All physics, fault signatures, warning thresholds and test catalog are constructed in the same synthetic emulator. This is **neither external validation nor demonstration of real-world fault recall**.
2. Procedures are not faithful to real EGSE electrical interfaces, qualification environments or time scales. No actual spacecraft data calibration.
3. Mode setup happens instantaneously; test duration is a fixed catalog budget, not measured wall time or a full test-operation model.
4. Requirement checks use 10-second samples for the active procedure only; physical limit violations can occur between samples, and test results are reported at the procedure end.
5. Guard/watchdog/payload tests include synthetic active probes. A blocked unrelated subsystem yields inconclusive checks, which may reduce the detection rate of an otherwise observable fault.
6. No retesting of the same requirement or repeated campaign phases; this is important for delayed and intermittent fault detection.
7. Model-fixed and adaptive maximize partly different objectives; this initial comparison does not isolate the **causal incremental value of telemetry feedback**. Introduce a no-telemetry adaptive ablation, explicitly comparable stopping/backfilling rules, and stronger policies.
8. Only 10 seeds; no confidence intervals yet. Statistical interpretation of these highly structured experiments should be very cautious.

## Planned v0.4 scientific upgrade

- Add temporal monitoring/retest scheduling and a *no-telemetry adaptive* ablation.
- Evaluate held-out severity/onset grids (including after t=600 and intermittent faults), environmental changes, and stochastic faults.
- Match fixed/random and adaptive stop/backfilling semantics or show both baseline variants.
- Report per-fault and per-condition detection, paired confidence intervals (and appropriate independence assumptions), false fails, and coverage/time Pareto curves.
- Extend website to show a synchronized comparison of two policy runs against the same scenario definition with the cumulative verification evidence over simulated time.

All numerical results are synthetic software outputs, **not estimated failure rates of real spacecraft**.
