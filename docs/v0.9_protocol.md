# SAT-V&V v0.9 — Experimental and integration protocol

## Research question
Does test prioritization from learned failure scores outperform hand-designed temporal and Bayesian information-gain schedulers when fault mechanisms recur, combine, or change after campaign start?

## Method
- **Plant:** v0.8 synthetic coupled EPS/thermal/OBC/COMMS/payload/1-axis ADCS emulator. Battery resistance adds voltage sag and I²R heat; actuator activity consumes modeled electric power.
- **Requirement catalog:** T01–T20, including four ADCS tests. Check status is PASS, FAIL, or INCONCLUSIVE. A verified FAIL ends the campaign; root cause inference does not determine PASS/FAIL.
- **Bayesian policy:** offline-smoothed outcome likelihoods (training seeds 301–303/451–453), approximate expected entropy reduction plus requirement novelty, age, criticality, and measured warning values.
- **Learned risk policy:** RandomForestClassifier (80 trees, max depth 8, min leaf 3). Features observed before a proposed test: test ID/cost/criticality, time, current V/I/T/sensor differences, packet loss, SOC, operating mode, previous-check age, prior executions, and nominal-voltage residual. No hidden fault label or future measurements among input features. Training seeds 171–173 only. Model scores are **not calibrated probabilities**.
- **Baselines:** fixed catalog and prior temporal-adaptive scheduler.
- **Held-out scenarios:** healthy with eclipse, healthy eclipse + load change, dual resistance+sensor fault, heater+radio bursts, wheel+battery, slow resistance fault, radio+gyro bias. Novel combinations use the same underlying uncalibrated physics.
- **Evaluation seeds:** 801–804, budgets 20, 25, 30 and 45 simulated minutes. Each seed/scene/policy uses its own simulator instance with equivalent initial specified conditions.
- **Metrics:** whether an executed test failed, time spent, healthy false-failure rate, and clustered percentile bootstrap uncertainty across distinct scenario families. Different test orders necessarily induce different states; this is inherent to the online-control research question, not a paired identical-trajectory experiment.

## Threats to validity
1. Only five faulty and two healthy held-out scenario *families*: uncertainty is high and bootstrap intervals do not ensure external validity.
2. Changing a scenario's label alone does not change controller behavior, but injected mechanisms are drawn from a small known simulator family. Data leakage across windows is avoided through seed-family partitions, but all data is synthetic.
3. The 1D attitude model omits orbit dynamics, spacecraft geometry, disturbance torques and real sensors.
4. Synthetic fault onset is scenario-scripted; failure labels are for evaluation only. A diagnostic posterior over single-fault hypotheses can be wrong on compound faults.
5. First requirement failure is not proof of correct root-cause attribution; early termination loses diagnostic information.
6. No empirical false-alarm guarantee can be claimed from the small healthy-control sample.
7. Scores from the learned ranker are uncalibrated and lack risk-sensitive safety certification. No unrestricted exploratory tests should be run on physical spacecraft.
8. One-step operating assumptions, simplified time costs and handcrafted subsystem test limits can favor particular policies.

## Required future research
- Independent models beyond the project simulator; additional operationally realistic healthy negatives and unseen component degradation curves.
- Nested training/calibration/evaluation and model-probability calibration, selective-prediction coverage, uncertainty under shifts.
- Battery electrothermal parameter calibration, quaternion attitude dynamics, torque constraints, recovery sequences and formal test invariants.
- Compare matched-false-alarm detection lead time, root-cause top-k accuracy, test costs, unsafe action count and energy margin—not detection only.
- Real measured datasets may test passive monitoring, but cannot validate counterfactual chosen diagnostic actions without an interactive bench.
